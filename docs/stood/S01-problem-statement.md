# S01: Problem statement

**One plot. One payer abroad. One hold with no reason.**

Ama is a nurse in London. She is paying, in stages, for a house on a plot in Accra. She sends money for the foundation. WhatsApp photos arrive for two months, then stop. Excuses start. Fourteen months later she flies home to a half-finished house, and the money is gone. She has no record of what was paid against what.

When someone in this corridor tries the "official" route, it fails in a different way. PayPal's January 2026 return to Nigeria through **Paga** let people receive money. But real payments were **held for weeks**, accounts were **limited or closed** right after users uploaded the documents asked for, and linking failed on name mismatches. The person got no reason, just a balance that didn't arrive.

**These are the same failure from two sides:** money moves without a reason attached. In one case it's released with no proof. In the other it's held with no explanation.

## The problem, in one sentence

> A payer abroad cannot make the next payment depend on proof that the work happened, and when a payment is held, nobody is told why.

## Why now

- Remittances to Africa are about $95–100B a year. The rails that move money are solved (Flutterwave, LemFi, NALA, M-Pesa). **Nothing checks what the money bought once it lands.**
- Agents are starting to pay without a human present (AP2 v0.2, Muse). Signed mandates prove *who* approved, not *whether it was right* (Whisper attacks, Sept 2026).
- PayPal is building PayPal World and partner corridors into Africa ([07](../07-paypal-landscape.md), [11](../11-africa-payments-and-eyeonsite.md)). The Paga launch showed that a corridor without a reason attached to each payment repeats the old holds.

## What Paga is in this document

**A contrast, not a competitor.** Stood doesn't replace Paga, Flutterwave or any rail. Paga linked two accounts. Stood ties a payment to a plot.
